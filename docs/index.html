Monthly Summary

At 1,038 TWh, July 2026 demand rose 2.9 percent year-on-year, covered almost entirely by domestic generation with a supply gap of barely 2 TWh. Solar reached 158 TWh for the first time, drawing level with hydro, while coal's monthly share edged back up to 51 percent after falling below 50 percent in the first half. The driving disruption remains the Hormuz crisis: since the Iran war erupted in late February, crude oil imports fell 13.2 percent in cumulative terms through July. The month itself showed a partial recovery to 35.7 million tonnes, still roughly 41 percent below the prior year. Coal imports rose 4.3 percent year-to-date as domestic production dropped 10.1 percent in July alone.

Capacity Added — H1 2026

In the first six months of 2026, China added 158.7 GW of new power capacity, equivalent to roughly two-thirds of Germany's entire installed electricity base. Solar remained the dominant contributor at 72.1 GW, though down 66 percent from a record 2025 baseline as developers rushed to complete projects ahead of a policy deadline governing existing price-support arrangements. Wind additions reached 38.6 GW, down 25 percent from the prior year but still higher than any full calendar year before 2025. Thermal capacity surged 49 percent to 38.4 GW, outpacing generation growth and driving plant utilisation rates lower. Hydro added 6 GW, up 53 percent, and nuclear returned with 3.6 GW after contributing nothing in the same period last year.

In June alone, wind led all sources with 13.6 GW, up 153 percent year-on-year and a record high for the month. The steep increase reflects a slump in June 2025 caused by the same policy deadline that compressed solar additions earlier in the year. Solar eased to 12.5 GW, down 12 percent. Thermal fell 27 percent to 6 GW. Hydro added 1.9 GW, up 205 percent. No new nuclear capacity was commissioned.
